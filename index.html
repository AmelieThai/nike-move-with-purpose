02 / The target audience

REAL LIFE.
REAL MOTIVATION.

Meet Alex, a fictional 21-year-old university student. Alex wants to get active with friends but struggles with time, confidence and a limited budget. These are campaign hypotheses, not survey findings.

THE GOAL.

Start running regularly. Spend time with friends. Build a routine that fits around classes.

THE FRICTION.

“I don't have time. I'm not fast enough. What if I buy the wrong shoes?” Budget and confidence come first.

THE SPARK.

A friend's invitation. A short campus run. A clear product introduction, without pressure to perform.

03 / Our proposed marketing strategy

THE GAME
PLAN.

The idea: make starting feel social, not intimidating. This SWOT is a proposed assessment for our student audience, not Nike's corporate strategy.

STRENGTHS ↗

Put the Nike identity and a clearly named running product at the centre of a memorable campus campaign.

WEAKNESSES ↘

A first shoe purchase may feel expensive or confusing to a student. Our message must explain the product without implying it is essential to participate.

OPPORTUNITIES ✳

Build the concept around campus run clubs, student ambassadors, friend invitations and relatable after-class stories.

THREATS ⚡

Students may choose another brand, a lower-priced pair or their existing shoes. Generic advertising could be ignored.

05 / The classroom pitch

ONE FRIEND.
ONE FIRST STEP.

Our campaign introduces the Nike Pegasus 42 to students like Alex, who want to start running but lack time and confidence. Our insight is simple: starting feels easier with a friend. “Your next class: outside” turns the end of the school day into an invitation to move. We propose campus posters, short social videos and a beginner-friendly group run. The aim is to connect a product with a relatable routine, not pressure students to perform or buy. We would measure engagement, registrations and product-page clicks to test whether the idea works.
